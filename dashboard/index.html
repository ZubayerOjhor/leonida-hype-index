How to correlate hype with a stock without fooling yourself

Attention and price both drift over three years, so comparing them raw can make a link appear or vanish by accident. The fix: compare surprises (today vs the usual level), not levels.

Method & sources

How it was built

Data (all public, free)

  • Attention: Wikimedia pageviews API, English article "Grand Theft Auto VI", users only, daily. The article went live on 4–5 Dec 2023.
  • Market: Yahoo Finance daily TTWO and SPY, adjusted close and volume.
  • Search: Google Trends "GTA 6", weekly, worldwide and Bangladesh; country ranking for the last 12 months.
  • Launch facts: third-party reports, linked on each event. Sales figures are Sensor Tower estimates, not Take-Two numbers.

Method

  • Event study: market model (TTWO on SPY) fitted on trading days −130 to −10; abnormal return on the reaction day (news after the close counts the next day).
  • Volume and attention ratios: reaction day vs the median of the previous 60 trading days / 30 calendar days.
  • Correlation: Pearson and Spearman on daily surprises (log value minus its trailing baseline); weekend views roll into Monday.
  • Wanted level: 20-day volatility of TTWO, ranked against its own 2023–26 history (5 stars = top 5%).

Caveats

  • Only about 10 events. Treat averages as stories, not laws.
  • Correlation is not causation: earnings news and hype often land on the same day.
  • Wikipedia attention skews to English-reading audiences.
