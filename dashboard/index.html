GTA VI is the most hyped video game ever made. It finally came out on 19 November 2026. The company that owns it sells shares on the stock market, so investors were watching too.

When fans got excited, did investors get excited too?

Short answer
Not really. Fans went wild for trailers. Investors only reacted to news about money: delays, sales and reviews.

The whole market

The S&P 500, 500 big US companies together. On a day when every share went up, Take-Two going up isn't GTA news. So we always ask: did Take-Two do better or worse than the market?

How do you measure hype? We count how many people read the GTA VI page on Wikipedia each day, and how often people searched "GTA 6" on Google. More readers and searches = more people curious about the game.

07 · How we know

Where the numbers come from

Data (all free and public)

  • Interest: daily readers of the English Wikipedia page "Grand Theft Auto VI" (the page went live with Trailer 1 in Dec 2023).
  • Share prices: Take-Two (TTWO) and the S&P 500 fund (SPY), daily, from Yahoo Finance.
  • Searches: Google Trends for "GTA 6", worldwide and Bangladesh.
  • Sales and reviews: news reports, linked on each moment. Sales are estimates, not official numbers.

Things to keep in mind

  • There are only about ten big moments, so treat the averages as a story, not a law.
  • Two things happening together doesn't prove one caused the other.
  • Wikipedia readers lean towards English speakers.
  • Fan project. Not linked to Rockstar Games or Take-Two. Not investment advice.
For the data-curious: the methods and terms

Methods

  • Event study. "Vs the market" is the abnormal return: Take-Two's daily return minus what a market model (TTWO regressed on SPY) predicts, fitted on trading days −130 to −10 before each event. News after the US close counts on the next trading day.
  • Trading and interest (× normal). That day's volume ÷ median of the previous 60 trading days; that day's Wikipedia views ÷ median of the previous 30 days.
  • Surprises, not levels. Section 05 correlates the log of each day's value minus its trailing baseline, because both raw series trend over time and would give a misleading answer. Weekend views roll into Monday.
  • Wanted level. 20-day volatility of Take-Two's daily returns (annualised), ranked against its own 2023–26 history: 5 stars = top 5%.

Terms

  • r (correlation): how closely two things move together, from 0 (not at all) to 1 (perfectly). Roughly: under 0.1 none, 0.1–0.3 weak, 0.3–0.5 moderate, above 0.5 strong.
  • Pearson vs Spearman: two ways to measure r. Spearman only looks at the order of values, so a few huge days count less.
  • Lag: comparing today's hype with trading a few days later (or earlier) to see which moves first.
